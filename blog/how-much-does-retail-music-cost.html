How Much Does Retail Music Actually Cost? The Real Math

Daniel Fox · April 2026 · 9 min read

TL;DR: Basic commercial music services cost $17 to $80 per location per month. But the subscription is the smallest line item. Add PRO licensing ($500 to $2,000 per location per year if not bundled), hardware ($500 to $3,500 installed), and the revenue cost of music that does not drive behavior, and a 10-location retailer is spending $12,000 to $25,000 annually on audio with no way to measure whether it helps.

Most retailers can tell you what they spend on lighting fixtures per store. They can tell you what the visual merchandising budget was last quarter. Ask what the store music costs and you will get a pause, followed by a number that probably leaves out half the actual expense.

The subscription fee is the number people know. The total cost of operating an in-store audio environment is larger, and almost nobody tracks it as a single line item.

Subscription Costs in 2026

The commercial music service market has consolidated around a few price bands. Here is where the major providers land as of April 2026.

Pandora CloudCover: $16.95 per month per location when prepaid annually. $19.95 month to month. This is the floor for compliant commercial music. Includes licensing, 300+ curated stations, explicit content filters, and basic scheduling. Designed for single-location or small multi-unit operators who need the problem solved at minimal cost.

StoreStreams: $20 per month per location. Similar feature set to CloudCover with a slightly different interface. Another budget option for SMBs.

SiriusXM for Business: $26.95 per month for the base tier. Enterprise pricing is negotiated, typically $40 to $80 per location per month depending on location count and feature requirements. 200+ channels, Pandora artist stations, scheduling tools, multi-location management.

Soundtrack Your Brand: $35 per month per location. $119 per month for up to five locations on their multi-unit plan. Over 100 million tracks, business-only channels, remote management. The Spotify-adjacent option for retailers who want a large catalog.

Mood Media: $30 to $80 per month per location. Pricing is not transparent. Contracts are typically three to five years with early termination penalties. The per-location cost depends on bundled services (hardware, scent marketing, digital signage) and negotiated terms. Getting an apples-to-apples number requires reading the full contract.

The Licensing Layer Most Retailers Miss

Some commercial services bundle all necessary performance licenses into the subscription fee. Some do not. The distinction matters because the licensing costs are not trivial.

Three performing rights organizations in the United States administer public performance licenses: ASCAP, BMI, and SESAC. If your music service does not explicitly include blanket licenses from all three, you need to secure them independently.

ASCAP's retail license runs approximately $400 to $900 per year for a single location, depending on square footage. BMI's retail license is similar. SESAC negotiates individually. For a single location, the combined PRO cost is roughly $800 to $2,000 per year on top of whatever streaming subscription you are paying.

For a 10-location chain, the annual PRO licensing cost is $8,000 to $20,000 if the fees are not bundled into your service subscription. This is money that shows up on no marketing budget, no operations report, and no retail P&L as a single line item. It is fragmented across multiple vendor invoices and frequently managed by whoever in the organization happened to receive the first ASCAP letter.

Services like CloudCover, Soundtrack Your Brand, and SiriusXM for Business include blanket licensing. Mood Media typically bundles it. But if you are using a service that does not explicitly state 'all performance licenses included,' verify before assuming.

Hardware

A streaming subscription assumes you have something to stream it through. Most commercial music services run on a dedicated media player, a tablet, or a connected device. The speakers, amplifier, and wiring are your responsibility.

For a single retail location, a basic commercial audio setup (ceiling speakers, amplifier, wiring, installation) runs $1,500 to $3,500. Higher-end systems with zone control and better speaker quality push toward $5,000 to $8,000. Many retailers already have speaker infrastructure from previous tenants or buildouts, which reduces this to the cost of a media player and possibly an amplifier.

For retailers opening new locations, the audio infrastructure cost is baked into the buildout budget. For retailers switching music services in an existing store, hardware costs are often zero or limited to a new streaming device ($50 to $200).

The Full Cost for a 10-Location Retailer

Here is what the total annual cost looks like for a 10-location specialty retailer using a mid-tier commercial music service.

Subscription: 10 locations at $35 per month = $4,200 per year. Range across providers: $2,000 (CloudCover annual) to $9,600 (SiriusXM enterprise).

Licensing: $0 if bundled with subscription. $8,000 to $20,000 per year if not bundled. Most mid-tier and enterprise services bundle. Budget-tier services sometimes do not.

Hardware: $0 for existing infrastructure with a new streaming device ($500 to $2,000 total across 10 locations). $15,000 to $35,000 for full new installs.

Total annual range: $2,500 (budget service, existing hardware, bundled licensing) to $30,000+ (enterprise service, full install, unbundled licensing). Most 10-location retailers land between $5,000 and $15,000 per year.

The Cost Nobody Counts

Every number above describes the cost of having music in the store. None of them describe the cost of having the wrong music.

Milliman's 1982 study measured a 38% difference in gross sales between slow and fast tempo conditions in the same grocery store. The dwell time effect has been replicated dozens of times since. Areni and Kim showed that classical music in a wine store shifted customers toward more expensive bottles without changing total units sold. North, Hargreaves, and McKendrick demonstrated that French music doubled French wine sales relative to German music.

The mechanism is consistent across studies: congruent music increases dwell time, raises willingness to pay, and shifts purchase behavior toward premium products. Incongruent music does the opposite. It primes customers away from purchasing.

If your store is running a $35 per month music service on a mood channel that does not match your customer's psychology, the subscription fee is the smallest cost you are paying. The larger cost is the revenue you are leaving because the audio environment is working against the rest of your store design.

The research consistently shows an 8 to 12 percent increase in willingness to pay when music matches customer identity. For a store doing $50,000 in monthly revenue, an 8% lift is $4,000 per month. $48,000 per year. From one variable that most retailers treat as a fixed condition.

What Measurement Would Change

The reason nobody calculates the cost of wrong music is that nobody measures the effect of any music. The commercial services listed above track what was scheduled to play. They do not track what happened while it played.

If you cannot connect your audio environment to dwell time, conversion, or average transaction value, you cannot calculate whether the music is a cost or an investment. And if you cannot calculate that, you will always treat it as a cost. Something to minimize rather than something to refine.

That measurement gap is the real expense. Not the $35 per month.
