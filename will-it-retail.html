Even A-tier songs weren't made for your floor

Every song was written for the artist, not for your customer. The tempo might be right, but the energy is wrong. The vibe fits, but the lyrics pull attention toward themselves. The best song on Monday morning is the wrong song on Saturday afternoon.

What each tier actually means

The case against every tier except one

A
Close, but made for someone else

Great songs that happen to work in a store. But “happens to work” means you’re getting lucky — not getting strategic.

B
Safe and forgettable

Nothing offensive, nothing memorable. Background noise that checks a box but never moves a needle.

C
Too much personality

The song becomes the main character. Customers notice the music instead of the merchandise. That’s a problem.

D
Creates the wrong state entirely

The energy is real — but it’s working against you. These songs put customers into motion: faster pace, shorter visits, earlier exits. Great music for a gym. Wrong music for a store.

F
Actively driving people out

Wrong tempo, wrong energy, wrong everything. Your playlist just cost you a sale — and you’ll never know it.

Made for the room

Built at the parameter level — tempo, energy, harmonic density, rhythmic feel — to do specific work in your specific environment. The only tier that actually belongs in a store.
