The measurement layer for retail audio.

Every retail store plays music. None of it is measured. We're closing the loop.

The thesis

Forty years of peer-reviewed research shows that music moves the variables retailers care about: dwell time, willingness to pay, conversion, basket size. The findings are consistent, well-replicated, and largely ignored by the companies selling music to retail.

What doesn't exist is a commercial dataset mapping specific musical composition parameters to verified retail outcomes at scale. The academic literature tells us tempo matters, genre congruence matters, mode matters. Nobody has ever measured, in real stores, which tempo, which harmonic structure, which lyrical priming, which combination of proprietary variables produces lift against a control.

Entuned is a behavioral data company that generates AI music specified at the variable level and deploys it in retail environments wired with existing sensor stacks. We are not a music company that captures data. We are a data company that outputs music.

A small category we displace. A much larger one we ride on top of. The opportunity is the intersection, not either market alone.

Entuned directly replaces the commercial background music line item on a retailer's P&L — a global category growing at 6.7% CAGR through 2030. That is the floor, not the ceiling. The real story is that we sit on top of the in-store analytics market, which is growing at 21–23% a year as retailers instrument their stores with RetailNext, ShopperTrak, and similar sensor platforms. Those stores are already collecting behavioral data they cannot act on. Entuned is the action layer.

The streaming giants have the music. The sensor companies have the data. Nobody has connected them. That gap is the company — and even a 1% reliable, repeatable lift applied across retail audio is GDP-moving.

What we're building

Entuned generates original music specified at the variable level — tempo, mode, harmonic density, rhythmic feel, timbral texture, lyrical priming — based on a psychographic profile of the store's ideal customer. The music is delivered through our adaptive player and correlated against in-store behavioral data from retail analytics platforms. Every store-hour of playback generates a new row in a dataset that has never existed.

We call the compositional parameters flow factors. Each track in the library is tagged across the full taxonomy. Each hour of in-store playback produces correlation data against foot traffic, dwell time, zone transitions, and point-of-sale outcomes. The feedback loop closes automatically.

The more stores in the network, the sharper the correlation model. The sharper the model, the more lift we deliver. The more lift we deliver, the more stores we win. The dataset compounds — and it is the asset, not the player.

The moat

Compound data is the only real defensibility in this category. Dozens of musical parameters × real retail environments × time equals a dataset that has never existed. No competitor can replicate years of cross-retail musical correlation data without years of access to real stores. The catalog players do not generate to spec. The consumer generators have no retail integrations. The sensor companies do not make music.

Provisional patent filed April 2026 covering the closed-loop architecture that connects parameterized music generation to verified retail outcomes. The specific flow factor taxonomy, ICP-to-composition translation logic, and state recipes are protected as trade secrets layered on top of the patent. Competitors starting today face an 18–24 month head start that compounds with every pilot deployed.
