For Apparel & Footwear Retail

High traffic, conversion-obsessed, multi-decision visits.

Apparel and footwear customers move fast, try a lot, and decide on sensory evidence in the first ten seconds. The audio shaping that decision is the one variable nobody on the floor team measures.

The Gap

Audio is doing work in the room. Almost nobody chose what.

Off-price apparel and multi-decision footwear share an operating shape: high foot traffic, conversion as the load-bearing metric, fitting-room dwell that varies by category and weather, and a shopper who is comparing options against price-relative-to-what-else-is-on-the-rack. The store environment shaping those decisions is being delivered by an audio vendor who has never been in your store.

Your visual merchandising team is well-staffed. Your real estate team has a model. Store ops owns staffing rotation. The audio environment those teams operate inside is whatever the music vendor sent over.

What the Research Says

Forty years of evidence on the levers that matter for high-traffic apparel.

Tempo affects pace and basket.

Milliman (1982) measured slower music increasing dwell. Knoferle et al. (2011) showed the effect depends on tempo paired with the right tonal choice — most catalogue vendors have the thermostat without the room.

Music customers read as theirs moves what they spend.

Areni and Kim (1993) measured average transaction value rising 8–12% when music matched the customer profile. North et al. (1999) replicated across categories. Same items on the rack, different customer read.

Familiar songs cut perceived dwell.

Yalch and Spangenberg (2000) found that familiar music made shoppers leave 8% sooner. They reported enjoying it more. They left faster. Catalogue vendors play familiar songs because complaints are minimized — that is the wrong objective for conversion.

Mismatched music actively reduces basket.

Andersson et al. (2012) ran 601 real transactions and found that music that clashes with the store cuts willingness to pay. Neutral is not the baseline. The audio environment is doing work in the room whether you chose its effect or not.
