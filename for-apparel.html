Every surface in your store is curated. The soundtrack shouldn't be the exception.

The fixtures are specified. The lighting temperature is specified. The mannequins were re-styled last week. A song comes on that does not belong in this store, and nobody on the floor knows who to ask about it because the playlist lives on somebody else's laptop.

What We Measure

Apparel shoppers decide whether a store is for them inside the first ten seconds, and they are deciding on sensory evidence. Yalch and Spangenberg (2000) found that familiar music actually made shoppers leave 8 percent faster. Andersson et al. (2012) watched 601 real transactions and found that music that clashes with the store cuts what customers spend. Your team measures conversion, average ticket, and dwell every week. The audio layer shaping those numbers is the one thing in the store nobody has a report on.

Dwell Time by Department

How long shoppers spend in each department. Browsers linger. Browsers buy. Familiar hits pull customers out of the quiet attentive state where they pick up a second item, so the background music for a considered apparel purchase has to do a different job than the music on a friend's party playlist.

Average Transaction Value

Whether the shopper adds the second item. Andersson et al. (2012) measured willingness to pay against music that did not fit the store. Customers spent less, and they spent less without being able to say why.

Brand-Music Congruence

Whether shoppers hear your brand or hear a contradiction. When a song with violent or sexually explicit lyrics plays in a store positioning itself as refined, the customer feels the disconnect before they can name it. Original music built for your brand removes that risk entirely because nothing on the speakers came from a catalog you did not curate.

Repeat Visit Rate

Whether shoppers come back. Customers who feel comfortable in a space return to it. Your staff hear the same playlist for roughly 2,000 hours a year, and a short loop means the same songs dozens of times a month. That wear shapes the energy on the floor, and customers read it.

How Entuned Works for Apparel Retail

We start with your customer. Not their age bracket or their zip code, but who they actually are, what they actually care about, what makes them feel like this store is for them. From there we build original music that fits that person and fits this store.

Every track is original, and there is no licensing exposure. No ASCAP bill, no PRO audit, no risk that a lyric in the background is undercutting the brand message on the wall.

We work with retail analytics platforms where you already have them deployed. When we put a new piece of music on the floor and dwell time moves in the same store the same week, you are looking at a measurement connected to a decision, not a vendor anecdote.
