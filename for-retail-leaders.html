You hired a lighting designer. Why not a sound designer?

Your team spent months on lighting, fixtures, and merchandising. Your soundtrack is a two-year-old Spotify playlist nobody remembers choosing.

Your Store Has a Blind Spot

You have walked into your own store and heard a song that did not belong. Lyrics that cut against the brand. Energy that did not match the customer in front of you. You noticed. Your customers noticed too.

Visual Design is Intentional

Every piece of visual merchandising reflects the brand. The lighting is calculated for ambiance. The color palette sends a message. The store is a carefully composed environment.

Audio is an Afterthought

The soundtrack is a Spotify playlist someone picked two years ago, or generic content from Mood Media or Soundtrack Your Brand, organized by mood-tags that have nothing to do with the customer walking through the door. Nobody screened the lyrics. Your brand has no control over what words come out of the speakers.

"Upbeat" is not a customer. "Indie rock" is not a strategy.

Generic playlists carry real risk. Unscreened lyrics can cut against your brand values. A playlist built for "chill vibes" does not distinguish between a 22-year-old browsing clearance and a 45-year-old buying a sofa. Your employees hear it for roughly 2,000 hours a year, 10x more than any customer. A short loop means they hear the same songs dozens of times a month.

What Changes With Entuned

We Learn Your Customer

We start with your customer. Not their age bracket, not their zip code. Who they actually are, what they actually care about, what makes them feel like this store is their store.

We Create Original Music

Every track is built specifically for that person and that store. Nothing is pulled from a catalog. Every lyric is intentional. Every word aligns with your brand.

Your Audio Becomes as Intentional as Your Visuals

The same rigor you apply to merchandising, lighting, and design now runs on the speakers. Every element aligned. One coherent experience.

Original Music, Built for Your Store

Generic playlists were built for a generic store that does not exist. Your customer is specific. Their aesthetic is specific. What makes them feel like they are in the right place is specific. Original music built for your store is the only way to answer that specificity, because a catalog was never built around a single customer to begin with.

How to Get Buy-In From Finance

The research gives your CFO the numbers. Areni and Kim (1993) found that congruent music increased willingness to pay. North, Hargreaves, and McKendrick (1999) measured meaningful increases in dwell time and category conversion when music matched the customer. Milliman (1982) tied music tempo directly to time spent in store. Peer-reviewed. Multiple retail categories. Decades of consistent direction.
