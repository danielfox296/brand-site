The Customer
Meet Mara.

She arrives on Saturday afternoons, usually after brunch. She’s in no hurry. She touches everything she’s considering. She’s not deciding whether she can afford it — she’s deciding whether it belongs in her life. She responds to music that doesn’t demand her attention. She finds busy soundscapes irritating.

Typical visit 18–24 minutes
Frequency 2× per month, Saturday afternoons
Decision style Deliberate — needs space to feel certain
Audio response Responds to sound that doesn’t compete for attention
Peak engagement When the environment feels unhurried

“She stays 22% longer when the room feels unhurried.”

What the system learned

Mara needs acoustic simplicity during decision windows. The system reduces rhythmic density when dwell time exceeds 60 seconds. It never introduces percussion during extended product engagement. The music becomes the room — not something in it.

The Story

Mara’s 18-minute visit.

The music shifted four times. Each shift was a decision the system made in response to what she was doing. Scroll through her visit — or click any moment to jump ahead.

0:00
Entry

Mara walks in. The music is already present. It doesn’t announce itself. It just makes the room feel right — warm, unhurried, without any urgency signal.

Acoustic texture, 80 BPM. Major key, muted voicings. No percussion. A room that says you’re welcome without saying it.

Active Browse

She’s moving through the floor. She’s picked up three things. The system detected movement and engagement. The music shifted — same temperature, slightly more present.

Light rhythmic pulse, 94 BPM. The customer is in motion. The music keeps pace without leading.

11:15
Extended Dwell

She’s been at the ceramics display for 90 seconds. The system detected it. The music slowed. Warmer. No percussion. Less hurried. The moment belongs to her — not to the store, not to a sale.

68 BPM. Major sevenths and suspended chords. No urgency signal. This is the money track.

16:48
Exit Approach

She started moving toward the door. She hadn’t bought anything yet. The music shifted — not louder, not faster. Different. A subtle reengagement cue. A question not yet answered.

87 BPM. Slightly more textural. A melodic idea that doesn’t resolve. 14% of exit-approach sessions convert.
