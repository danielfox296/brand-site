The Problem

The music in most stores was never designed to support purchases.

Alcott sells elevated everyday objects. Ceramics, textiles, small furniture. Their customers don't rush. They browse with intent. They pick things up, put them down, pick them up again.

What music should be playing for them?

Right now, most stores like this have three options:

♫ The Playlist

A poorly curated playlist that no one is responsible for. Songs picked for taste, not outcomes.

⚠ Personal Spotify

The associate's account. Consumer licenses aren't cleared for commercial use. Every play is a violation.

◼ Big-Box Provider

Outsourced vibe. Thousands of stores get the same catalog. Nothing is built for your customer.

In all three cases, the content of the songs was never designed to support customer behavior. It just has to be inviting enough not to drive people out. That's the bar.

What Music Actually Does

Music does more than "not drive people out." Every track carries dozens of variables that shape behavior — most of them invisible in a typical setup:

Tempo

Affects walking pace. Slower tempos extend dwell time. Faster tempos clear the floor.

Key & Mode

Major keys lift mood. Minor keys create introspection. The wrong one changes the decision environment.

Instrumentation

Carries cultural meaning. Acoustic signals craft and care. Synths signal trend. Each shapes brand perception.

Lyrics

Words suggest things. A lyric about leaving primes departure. Lyrical content is never neutral.

Familiarity

When a customer hears a song she knows, she perceives time passing faster. She feels like she's been there longer. She wants to leave sooner.

None of these factors are deliberate in a typical retail music setup. They're incidental. Every song is a roll of the dice.

Ideal Customer Profile
The Customer
Mara.

She arrives on Saturday afternoons. She touches everything she's considering. She's not deciding whether she can afford it. She's deciding whether it belongs in her life.

She finds busy soundscapes irritating. She responds to music that doesn't demand her attention. She stays longer when the room feels unhurried.

What the music needs to do

Support deliberate browsing. Slow tempo, acoustic texture, no rhythmic urgency. The music should feel like the room, not like something playing in the room. She needs space to consider. The sound should give her that.
