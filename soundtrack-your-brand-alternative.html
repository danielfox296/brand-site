Credit where it's due

What Soundtrack gets right

Soundtrack has the largest commercially-licensed-for-business catalog on the market and a polished app. If "give my store the same music my customers already listen to, legally" is the strategy, they do it well. Three things they nail:

100M+ track catalog

Direct label deals after the Spotify spin-out, plus an AI Playlist Generator launched in 2025. Largest licensed-for-business library on the market.

Polished app experience

iOS, Android, web. Familiar pattern for operators who've used consumer streaming. Runs on commodity hardware or a phone.

Editorial curation

Real human editors investing in commercial-context playlists, not just algorithmic mixes. The output is genuinely well-shaped for retail.

For owner-operators

Where Soundtrack falls short

Four operator-level problems on multi-zone stores and brand-identity work.

Per-zone pricing penalizes real stores

Most retail floors aren't a single zone — sales floor + fitting room + patio + cafe annex. Soundtrack treats each as its own subscription. Multi-zone stores hit $90–$150/mo at one location.

Shared catalog, shared identity

The catalog Soundtrack curates from is the same catalog every other Soundtrack customer pulls from. Fine for ambience; problem for sonic identity.

No published lift methodology

Marketing leans on the general atmosphere literature, not on measurement inside their customers' stores. We couldn't find a published methodology.

You're licensing, not owning

If Soundtrack loses a label deal or restructures the catalog, the songs you've curated can disappear from your zones. Real risk on any licensed-catalog product.

What Entuned does instead

Free forever. Per-location, not per-zone. Original music we own outright that never appears in another brand's store. PRO-indemnified the moment it plays. Catalog can't disappear — we own it.

Common questions

Switching from Soundtrack Your Brand

Can I keep my Soundtrack account while testing Entuned?
Yes. Entuned Free is $0 with no credit card, so you can run it in one zone or one store as a parallel test while your Soundtrack subscription stays active. We recommend doing exactly that for a couple of weeks before you cancel anything — it makes the comparison concrete.
Will I owe ASCAP or BMI if I switch to Entuned?
No, not for music played through Entuned. We compose and own every track in our catalog outright, so there's no PRO obligation passed to the operator for audio Entuned delivers. Music played from any other source you run in the location keeps its own licensing posture.
How fast can I roll Entuned out across all my locations?
Each location takes minutes — the player is a URL on a browser-capable device. Multi-location operators self-serve through app.entuned.co/start and use the customer dashboard to manage all sites from one login. There's no hardware to ship and no install window to schedule.
Can I keep playlists I've curated in Soundtrack?
No, because the underlying tracks are licensed catalog from Soundtrack and aren't portable. What we do instead is interview your space, your customer, and your daypart structure, and engineer original programming that produces the same retail outcome — pace, energy arc, lyrical density, harmonic feel. Operators usually find this works better than the playlists they spent hours curating.
Is there a usage limit on Entuned Free?
Entuned Free is a real free tier — no credit card, no trial timer, intended for ongoing use by single-location operators who don't need the deeper customization or measurement of the paid tiers. The differences are documented on the pricing page; nothing on the free tier expires.
My customers love hearing recognizable songs. Won't original music feel weird?
It's a fair concern. The honest finding from operators who've made the switch is that customers don't consciously evaluate specific songs — they evaluate fit. Music that matches the store's pace and feel reads as "well put together," even when no one in the room could name a track.
