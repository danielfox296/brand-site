Soundtrack Your Brand Alternative

One subscription per location. Music your store actually owns.

Soundtrack Your Brand spun out of Spotify Business in 2018 and re-licensed a 100M-track catalog directly with the labels. It's a strong product. It's also priced per zone, per location — so a store with three zones is paying three subscriptions for music other brands also play. Entuned charges per location and the music is original, owned, and PRO-free.

What Soundtrack charges

Per zone, per month — one subscription per zone

Soundtrack bills per zone per month, with every zone needing its own subscription and player. Public list pricing isn't always reachable on soundtrack.io directly — we couldn't fetch their live pricing page on 2026-05-09 — but third-party listings have placed Soundtrack's tiers in roughly the $29/zone/mo range at the entry tier and up to around $54/zone/mo for the Unlimited tier, depending on whether you prepay annually or pay monthly. Operators should confirm the current rate directly with Soundtrack at the time of purchase.

Soundtrack offers a 14-day free trial, app-based playback on iOS, Android, and web, and an optional hardware player for around $200. They originally launched with Spotify-licensed catalog and now hold direct label deals. (Billboard, on the Spotify spin-out and re-licensing)

Credit where it's due

What Soundtrack gets right

Soundtrack has the largest commercially-licensed-for-business catalog on the market — over 100 million tracks — and a polished app experience that operators familiar with consumer streaming will recognize. They've invested in editorial curation, launched an AI Playlist Generator in 2025, and run cleanly on commodity hardware or a phone. If your strategy is "give my store the same music my customers already listen to, just legally," Soundtrack does that better than anyone else in the licensed-catalog space.

For owner-operators

Where Soundtrack falls short

  • Per-zone pricing penalizes real stores. Most retail floors aren't a single zone — there's a sales floor, a fitting room or chair area, a back patio, a cafe annex. Soundtrack treats each as its own subscription. A multi-zone store can easily run $90–$150/mo at one location alone.
  • Licensed catalog means shared identity. Soundtrack's catalog is the same catalog any other Soundtrack customer can program. Your store soundtrack is curated from a pool other brands also pull from. That's fine for ambience; it's a problem if you're trying to build sonic identity.
  • No published in-store sales-lift methodology. Soundtrack does not publish a sales-lift measurement methodology — we couldn't find one. Their marketing leans on the general atmosphere literature, not on measurement done inside their customers' stores.
  • You're licensing, not owning. If Soundtrack ever loses a label deal or restructures its catalog, the songs you've curated can disappear from your zones. That's a real risk on any licensed-catalog product.

Common questions

Switching from Soundtrack Your Brand

Can I keep my Soundtrack account while testing Entuned?
Yes. Entuned Free is $0 with no card, so you can run it in one zone or one store as a parallel test while your Soundtrack subscription stays active. We recommend doing exactly that for a couple of weeks before you cancel anything — it makes the comparison concrete.
Will I owe ASCAP or BMI if I switch to Entuned?
No, not for music played through Entuned. We compose and own every track in our catalog outright, so there's no PRO obligation passed to the operator for audio Entuned delivers. Music played from any other source you run in the location keeps its own licensing posture.
How fast can I roll Entuned out across all my locations?
Each location takes minutes — the player is a URL on a browser-capable device. Multi-location operators self-serve through app.entuned.co/start and use the customer dashboard to manage all sites from one login. There's no hardware to ship and no install window to schedule.
Can I keep playlists I've curated in Soundtrack?
No, because the underlying tracks are licensed catalog from Soundtrack and aren't portable. What we do instead is interview your space, your customer, and your daypart structure, and engineer original programming that produces the same retail outcome — pace, energy arc, lyrical density, harmonic feel. Operators usually find this works better than the playlists they spent hours curating.
Is there a usage limit on Entuned Free?
Entuned Free is a real free tier — no card, no trial timer, intended for ongoing use by single-location operators who don't need the deeper customization or measurement of the paid tiers. The differences are documented on the pricing page; nothing on the free tier expires.
My customers love hearing recognizable songs. Won't original music feel weird?
It's a fair concern. The honest finding from operators who've made the switch is that customers don't consciously evaluate specific songs — they evaluate fit. Music that matches the store's pace and feel reads as "well put together," even when no one in the room could name a track.
